ANALYSIS
========

1. Does Qwen best-of-k accuracy plateau before reaching Dream's level?

   Countdown: NO. The curve rises steeply throughout the range j=1..35 with no
   sign of plateau. The marginal gain from j=16 to j=32 is +10.6pp, indicating
   strong continued scaling. Qwen massively exceeds Dream (39.1% vs 6.6%) well
   before reaching the compute budget.

   Mini Sudoku: PARTIAL DECELERATION. The curve shows decelerating growth: gains
   of +15.6pp from j=16 to j=32, but only +4.7pp from j=32 to j=39. The curve
   has not fully plateaued, but the marginal returns are diminishing. At k=39,
   Qwen (67.2%) is still 10.4pp below Dream (77.6%).

2. Does Qwen best-of-k cross Dream's accuracy before or after the compute-matched k?

   Countdown: WELL BEFORE. Qwen crosses Dream's 6.6% at approximately j=3-4,
   requiring only ~10% of the compute-matched budget to surpass Dream. By the
   compute-matched k=35, Qwen is 32.5pp ahead of Dream.

   Mini Sudoku: DOES NOT CROSS by the compute-matched k. At k=39 (compute-matched),
   Qwen achieves 67.2%, still 10.4pp below Dream's 77.6%. The compute-matched
   budget is insufficient for the AR model to match diffusion on this task.

3. Approximate k at which Qwen would match Dream (extrapolation)?

   Countdown: Already matched at j~4. No extrapolation needed.

   Mini Sudoku: Log-linear regression (acc = 0.168 * ln(j) + 0.027, R on data
   j=1..39) estimates Qwen would reach 77.6% at approximately j=87. This is
   roughly 2.2x the compute-matched budget (k=39). While not trivially small,
   it is feasible (under 100 samples) and suggests the diffusion advantage on
   Sudoku could in principle be closed by spending ~2x more AR compute. However,
   this is extrapolation beyond the observed range and actual convergence may be
   slower due to diminishing returns.

SUMMARY
=======

The scaling behavior is task-dependent:

- On Countdown (arithmetic reasoning), Qwen best-of-k scales efficiently and
  surpasses Dream at j~4, long before compute matching. The curve shows no
  saturation. The diffusion model has no advantage here.

- On Mini Sudoku (constraint satisfaction), Qwen best-of-k scales consistently
  but cannot reach Dream's level within the compute-matched budget. The curve
  shows deceleration but no hard plateau, suggesting the gap could close with
  roughly 2x more compute (estimated k~87). This implies the diffusion advantage
  on Sudoku is real but not insurmountable -- it reflects a moderate efficiency
  gap rather than a fundamental capability difference.

The key insight: Dream's advantage on Sudoku appears to be a compute-efficiency
advantage (it solves the task in fewer "effective attempts") rather than a
capability ceiling that AR sampling cannot reach. The scaling curve has not
saturated, meaning more AR compute would continue to close the gap.
