     1	# FIR-CUSUM Optimization Report (Iteration 0)
     2	
     3	## Experiment Overview
     4	
     5	Optimized the CUSUM-epsilon controller by replacing the full reset (S_t=0) after rollback with a Fast Initial Response (FIR) partial reset (S_t = h * reset_fraction). This addresses the "reset blind spot" where the original CUSUM repeatedly accepted perturbed/corrupted steps because it had to re-accumulate evidence from scratch after each rollback.
     6	
     7	## Issue Diagnosed
     8	
     9	**Reset Blind Spot (Critical)**: After each rollback triggered by S_t > h, the original implementation reset S_t to 0. During ongoing perturbation, the CUSUM needed ~3 steps to climb back from 0 to h=14 before re-triggering. This caused an oscillating accept/reject pattern where ~6/10 perturbed steps were accepted (vs Or-epsilon's ~3.9/10). Each accepted perturbed step corrupted the model state further, causing escalating probe loss that persisted long after the perturbation window ended.
    10	
    11	## Fix Applied
    12	
    13	1. **FIR partial reset**: After rollback, set S_t = h * 0.5 instead of 0. This retains evidence of recent instability so the CUSUM re-triggers within 1-2 anomalous steps.
    14	2. **S_0 = 0 at initialization**: The FIR head start is only applied after rollback, not at training start, to avoid early false alarms during the naturally volatile early training phase.
    15	3. **Re-calibrated h**: With FIR reset in the calibration simulation, h=18.0 gives exact alarm rate=0.002 matching the target p_0.
    16	
    17	## Setup
    18	
    19	- Same model/data/perturbation as main experiment (ResNet-18/CIFAR-10)
    20	- CUSUMController with h=18.0, k=0.5, reset_fraction=0.5, alpha=0.1
    21	- mu_0=-0.0413, sigma_0=0.2260 (unchanged from calibration)
    22	- 20 seeds x 3 conditions (step, ramp, nominal)
    23	
    24	## Key Results

    31	| Excess AUC (ramp) | 271.37 +/- 109.07 | 211.95 +/- 68.58 | 114.60 +/- 51.35 | -22% |

    44	## Key Observations
    45	
    46	1. FIR partial reset substantially reduces peak excess loss (36% for step, 35% for ramp) by preventing the oscillating accept/reject pattern during perturbation.
    47	2. Excess AUC also improves (34% for step, 22% for ramp), reflecting faster recovery after perturbation.
