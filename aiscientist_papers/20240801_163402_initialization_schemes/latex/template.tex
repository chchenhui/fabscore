\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Transformer Models}
The Transformer architecture, introduced by \citet{vaswani2017attention}, has revolutionized deep learning, particularly in natural language processing. Transformers leverage self-attention mechanisms to process input sequences in parallel, allowing for more efficient training and better handling of long-range dependencies compared to recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). This architecture has been the foundation for many state-of-the-art models, including BERT \citep{Devlin2019BERTPO} and GPT \citep{radford2019language}.

\subsection{Initialization Schemes}
Initialization schemes play a crucial role in the training of neural networks. Proper initialization can lead to faster convergence and better performance, while poor initialization can hinder the training process. Several initialization methods have been proposed, including Xavier initialization \citep{Glorot2010UnderstandingTD}, He initialization \citep{he2015delving}, and more generic schemes like Uniform and Gaussian initialization. These methods aim to set the initial weights in a way that preserves the variance of the activations and gradients throughout the network layers.

\subsection{Hyperparameters in Neural Networks}
Hyperparameters are critical in determining the performance of neural networks. They include learning rates, batch sizes, and the number of layers, among others. The choice of hyperparameters can significantly affect the training stability, speed to convergence, and final model accuracy. Grid search and random search techniques are commonly used to find optimal hyperparameter settings \citep{Bergstra2012RandomSF}.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
In this work, we focus on the tasks of modular arithmetic and permutation, which are fundamental in theoretical computer science. We define the problem setting as follows: given two elements \(a\) and \(b\) from a set, the goal is to predict the result of an operation \(a \circ b\) under a specific modulus \(p\). For permutation tasks, the goal is to predict the permutation of a sequence given a permutation function. We assume that the input sequences are of fixed length and that the operations are well-defined and closed within the set.

These schemes are chosen based on their widespread use and theoretical foundations. Xavier initialization is designed to keep the scale of the gradients roughly the same in all layers, while He initialization is particularly suited for layers with ReLU activations. Uniform and Gaussian initializations are more generic and serve as baselines for comparison.

\subsection{Hyperparameters}
In addition to initialization schemes, we explore the impact of various hyperparameters, including learning rates, batch sizes, and the number of layers. Learning rates are critical for controlling the step size during optimization \citep{kingma2014adam}, while batch sizes affect the gradient estimates and training stability. The number of layers determines the model's capacity to learn complex patterns. We systematically vary these hyperparameters to identify configurations that lead to faster and more effective grokking.
