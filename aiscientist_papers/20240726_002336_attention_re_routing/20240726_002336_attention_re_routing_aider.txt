\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we describe our proposed method for enhancing transformer efficiency through dynamic attention re-routing based on historical relevance scores. We build on the concepts introduced in the Background and formalize the approach using the notation defined in the Problem Setting.

Our method involves dynamically adjusting the attention weights during training based on the historical relevance scores of tokens. The historical relevance score \( R(x_i) \) for each token \( x_i \) is computed as the running average of its attention weights over previous training iterations. This score is used to re-route attention from less relevant tokens to more relevant ones, thereby optimizing the allocation of computational resources.

To calculate the historical relevance scores, we maintain a dictionary where the keys are token indices and the values are the running averages of the attention weights. During each training iteration, we update the relevance score \( R(x_i) \) for each token \( x_i \) using the formula:
\[
R(x_i) = \alpha R(x_i) + (1 - \alpha) \text{attn\_weight}(x_i)
\]
where \( \alpha \) is a weighting factor that determines the influence of past attention weights. This approach ensures that the relevance scores are updated smoothly and reflect the long-term importance of tokens.

We modify the forward method of the CausalSelfAttention class to incorporate the historical relevance scores. Specifically, we adjust the attention weights by multiplying them with the relevance scores before applying the softmax function. This re-routing mechanism ensures that more relevant tokens receive higher attention weights, while less relevant tokens receive lower weights. The modified attention weights \( A' \) are computed as:
\[
A' = \text{softmax}(A \cdot R)
\]
where \( A \) is the original attention weight matrix and \( R \) is the matrix of relevance scores.

The dynamic attention re-routing mechanism is integrated into the training loop. During each iteration, we log relevant statistics such as changes in attention distribution and training dynamics. This logging helps us analyze the impact of the re-routing mechanism on model performance and efficiency.
