\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates the impact of predictive uncertainty on the grokking phenomenon in neural networks, particularly within the context of modular arithmetic and permutation groups. Grokking, the unexpected generalization of models after prolonged periods of overfitting, poses a significant challenge in understanding model behavior and training dynamics. We propose a novel approach to quantify predictive uncertainty using Monte Carlo dropout and entropy measures during inference. Our contributions include modifying the Transformer architecture to output both predicted classes and uncertainty measures, and updating training and evaluation protocols to log these values. Through extensive experiments, we analyze the correlation between predictive uncertainty and validation accuracy, providing insights into the model's learning dynamics and grokking behavior. Our results demonstrate that predictive uncertainty can serve as a valuable indicator of model performance and generalization, offering a new perspective on the grokking phenomenon. Specifically, we show that predictive uncertainty evolves over time and correlates with validation accuracy, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic tool for model training and evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Formalism}
\label{sec:formalism}
We denote the input space by \( \mathcal{X} \) and the output space by \( \mathcal{Y} \). The model \( f_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y} \) is trained to minimize a loss function \( \mathcal{L}(f_\theta(x), y) \). To quantify predictive uncertainty, we use Monte Carlo dropout during inference, where multiple forward passes are performed with dropout enabled. The predictive mean \( \hat{y} \), variance \( \sigma^2 \), and entropy \( H \) are computed as follows:
\begin{align}
    \hat{y} &= \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} f_\theta^{(t)}(x), \\
    \sigma^2 &= \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left(f_\theta^{\left(t\right)}(x) - \hat{y}\right)^2, \\
    H &= - \sum_{i=1}^{C} \hat{y}_i \log \hat{y}_i,
\end{align}
where \( T \) is the number of Monte Carlo samples, \( C \) is the number of classes, and \( f_\theta^{(t)}(x) \) denotes the \( t \)-th stochastic forward pass.
