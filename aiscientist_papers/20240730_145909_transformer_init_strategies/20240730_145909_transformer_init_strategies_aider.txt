In this work, we focus on the initialization of weights in transformer-based language models. Let $\mathbf{W}_l$ denote the weight matrix for layer $l$ in a transformer model, where $l \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}$ and $L$ is the total number of layers. The goal is to find an initialization strategy that ensures stable gradients and fast convergence during training.

% Specific assumptions and unusual aspects highlighted
Our approach assumes that the initialization should be sensitive to the layer's position within the network (depth-adaptive) and should incorporate prior knowledge about the attention mechanisms (task-specific priors). These assumptions deviate from the standard practices of uniform or random initialization and aim to tailor the initialization process to the specific requirements of transformer models.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% Overview of the methodological approach, building on the problem setting and background
In this section, we detail our novel initialization strategies for transformer-based language models, building upon the formalisms introduced in the Problem Setting (Section \ref{subsec:problem_setting}) and the foundational concepts discussed in the Background (Section \ref{sec:background}). Our approach is designed to address the limitations of traditional initialization methods, such as Xavier/Glorot \cite{goodfellow2016deep} and Kaiming/He \cite{goodfellow2016deep}, which do not account for the unique characteristics of transformer architectures.

% Description of depth-adaptive scaling
\subsection{Depth-Adaptive Scaling}
\label{subsec:depth_adaptive_scaling}
Depth-adaptive scaling is a strategy that adjusts the initialization of weights based on the layer's position within the network. This approach is motivated by the observation that the depth of a transformer model can significantly impact the propagation of gradients during training. By scaling the initialization variance inversely proportional to the square root of the layer's depth, we aim to stabilize the gradient flow and promote faster convergence. This method is inspired by the insights from \citet{vaswani2017attention} regarding the depth and attention mechanisms in transformers.

% Description of task-specific priors
\subsection{Task-Specific Priors}
\label{subsec:task_specific_priors}
Task-specific priors involve incorporating prior knowledge about the attention mechanisms into the initialization process. By leveraging the structure of the attention heads and the distribution of tokens in the training data, we tailor the initialization to better suit the specific requirements of the language modeling task. This approach is designed to improve the model's performance by facilitating the learning of meaningful representations from the outset. The concept of task-specific priors is influenced by the work on layer normalization \cite{ba2016layer} and the application of prior knowledge in neural network initialization.

% Implementation details and integration with the model
\subsection{Implementation and Integration}
\label{subsec:implementation}
We implement our initialization strategies within the GPT model architecture \cite{karpathy2023nanogpt}, modifying the \texttt{\_init\_weights} function to include options for depth-adaptive scaling and task-specific priors. These strategies are integrated into the model's initialization process, ensuring that they are applied consistently across all layers. The implementation leverages the PyTorch framework \cite{paszke2019pytorch} for flexibility and efficiency in training transformer-based language models.

We employed perplexity and F1 score as our primary evaluation metrics. Perplexity assesses how well the model predicts the test data, with lower values indicating better performance.     \item Dropout rate: 0.2

    \item Beta2: 0.99
    \item Gradient clipping: 1.0
